SharaSpot LinkedIn Playbook

Retuned for competitor-grade content marketing, not product promotion.

This version is rebuilt using the patterns visible in players like Instantly and Smartlead: category education, operator voice, diagnostics, proof assets, and infrastructure authority. The goal is to make SharaSpot feel sharper, more technical, and more trusted without sounding like a brochure.

Competitive Read

Pattern 1

They do not market a feature. They market a system.

Instantly packages benchmarks, infrastructure, deliverability, AI, and outbound workflows into a larger operating model.

Pattern 2

They publish operator content, not just brand content.

Smartlead uses engineer voice, support voice, founder voice, and customer voice to make the company feel deeper than a UI.

Pattern 3

They turn education into product gravity.

Bench reports, guides, university content, tools, and case studies all pull attention back into the product ecosystem.

What is working for them

  • Hard takes on why campaigns fail before they launch.
  • Diagnostic content that names hidden failure points.
  • Operator-led posts that sound like real internal knowledge.
  • Customer proof with scale, reply rate, or booked-meeting outcomes.
  • Resource ecosystems: reports, courses, tools, and case studies.

What SharaSpot should not copy

  • Empty AI hype without an operational angle.
  • Feature-drop posts with no thesis behind them.
  • Generic cold email advice that any creator can post.
  • High-volume bragging as the main proof of quality.

SharaSpot Positioning Shift

The company page should feel like it is run by people who understand why outbound breaks underneath the surface, not by people trying to collect likes with generic sales tips.

Old Risk

Pain-led launch content alone can make the brand sound early-stage and emotional, but not yet authoritative.

New Stance

SharaSpot should own the idea that outbound is an infrastructure discipline before it is a copywriting discipline.

Brand Role

Be the calm systems operator in a market full of send-more noise.

Content Doctrine

What Every Post Must Do
  • Teach something specific about why outbound succeeds or fails.
  • Carry one clean thesis, not five weak points.
  • Sound like an operator with pattern recognition.
  • Make the reader rethink their current workflow.
  • Leave room for product, but do not start with product.
Ideal Mix
  • 50% category and operator POV
  • 25% diagnostics and teardown content
  • 15% case-study or workflow proof
  • 10% direct product or launch content

Best Formats

Founder post, engineer post, teardown post, checklist carousel, customer proof slide, and system diagram screenshot.

Best Hooks

Use contrarian openings, hidden-failure openings, or "the problem started earlier" openings.

Worst Hooks

"We are excited to announce", "Here is a feature update", and generic motivational sales copy.

Execution Rules

Do This
  • Write like an operator who has seen the failure pattern before.
  • Use screenshots, diagrams, and mini-frameworks more than glossy brand graphics.
  • Let founder, engineering, and product voices rotate.
  • Reference hidden systems, not just visible features.
  • Build recurring series, for example: SharaSpot Signals, System Breakdown, or Inbox Reality.
Avoid This
  • "We are excited" openings.
  • Feature-first copy with no lesson behind it.
  • Generic cold-email tips that could belong to any LinkedIn creator.
  • Overusing emojis, hype, or exaggerated metrics.
  • Turning every post into a trial CTA.
